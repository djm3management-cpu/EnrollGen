Simplified Issue · No Medical Exam

Underwriting: What Agents Must Know

Shared Questions — MedMax & MedPerformance & MVP Pro

No exam. No labs. Yes on any = declination.

  • Q1: COBRA disabled or missed 10+ consecutive workdays due to illness/injury in 12 months
  • Q2: Scheduled for or advised to seek hospitalization or surgery in past 12 months
  • Q3: Pending test results, pending services, or currently pregnant / planning pregnancy in 12 months
  • Q5: Ongoing condition likely to cost $5,000+/year (verify with GoodRX)
Never coach a client to answer No when the accurate answer is Yes. Application fraud voids coverage and creates agent liability.

MedMax Q4 — Last 12 Months

Diagnosed or treated in the past 12 months for:

  • Cancer, heart/blood vessel disease, stroke
  • Kidney disease, diabetes, alcohol/drug abuse
  • Lung, liver, blood disorders
  • Mental/emotional/nervous system disorders
  • Arthritis, back, bones, or joint disorders

MedPerformance Q4: LIFETIME History

Has any applicant ever been diagnosed with:

  • Chronic kidney disease, kidney failure, dialysis
  • Type 1 diabetes or Type 2 with complications
  • Cancer (other than fully removed basal cell)
  • Heart attack, heart surgery, stroke, TIA, stent
  • HIV/AIDS, autoimmune (Crohn's, lupus, MS, RA)
  • ALS, Parkinson's, organ transplant
Lifetime lookback — the key pre-qualifying distinction from MedMax.

MedAccess MVP Basic — Near Guaranteed Issue

~99% Approval Rate

Only one underwriting question:

"Does any applicant have any medical test results pending, or a medical service or surgery that has not yet been performed?"

This is the only declination trigger. Almost all applicants qualify.

MedAccess MVP Pro — Simplified Issue

Same 5 questions as MedMax (Q1–Q3, Q4 last 12 months, Q5). Yes on any = declination.

  • Q1: COBRA disabled / missed 10+ consecutive workdays
  • Q2: Scheduled for hospitalization or surgery
  • Q3: Pending results or pregnant / planning pregnancy
  • Q4: Diagnosed/treated in last 12 months (cancer, heart, kidney, diabetes, lung, mental health, joint)
  • Q5: Ongoing condition likely to cost $5,000+/year

Agent Compliance

What to Say, and What Not to Say

❌ Never Say These

"This is the same as having Cigna insurance."

The plan uses the Cigna network. Claims are funded by the plan, not Cigna.

"This is an ACA plan."

These are ERISA-governed plans. Legally distinct from ACA marketplace coverage.

"Cancel your marketplace plan; this is the same thing for less."

Never make benefit equivalence claims. Subsidy reconciliation and ACA compliance require proper consultation.

"This is real major medical." (for MedMax)

MedMax is a defined-benefit PPO with annual utilization caps. Must be described accurately.

✓ Use This Language Instead

For MedPerformance

"MedPerformance is a self-funded ERISA major medical plan on the Cigna PPO network, with no lifetime benefit maximum, covers hospital, surgery, specialist, and prescriptions. Administered by Securus Benefits / Yuzu Health with A-rated stop-loss carrier backing."

For MedMax

"MedMax is a self-funded ERISA defined-benefit PPO on the First Health network. It has scheduled copay benefits with annual utilization limits I'll walk you through, and unlimited $0 telemedicine through RelyMD starting day one."

Sales Training

Handling the Most Common Objections

"Is this real insurance? I've been burned before."
This is a legitimate PPO health plan, not a discount card. It's on the First Health / Cigna network, you get a member ID card, and it pays claims through real network pricing. It's a defined-benefit plan, so let me walk you through the structure so you know exactly what you're getting.
"This isn't 'real' Cigna; can I use it at Cigna providers?"
You'll use it at Cigna in-network providers, same directory, same discounts. The distinction is that it's self-funded under ERISA, administered by Securus Benefits / Yuzu Health. Cigna provides the network; Securus Benefits / Yuzu Health handles the claims. Your provider interaction is identical.
"What if the plan goes under?"
Both plans carry stop-loss reinsurance through SiriusPoint, an A-rated carrier. The plan structure is the same framework used by large employers and unions to cover millions of Americans. It's built with layered financial protection, not open-ended exposure.
"Why not just get an ACA plan?"
If you qualify for a meaningful subsidy, the ACA may well be the right answer; we'll look at that first. These plans are specifically designed for clients who don't qualify for a subsidy, can't use the marketplace, or are between enrollment windows. That's a large and underserved population.
