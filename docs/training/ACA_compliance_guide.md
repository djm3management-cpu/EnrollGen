# ACA / Marketplace Agent Compliance & Call Guide

*For Internal Agent Training Only | Updated April 2026*

*All agents must follow current CMS/state exchange guidelines and carrier-specific requirements.*

---

## 1. ACA Fundamentals for Agents

Before getting on the phone, every agent needs to understand the core structure of the ACA marketplace.

### 1.1 What ACA Plans Are

ACA-compliant plans are Qualified Health Plans (QHPs) that cover all ten Essential Health Benefits (EHBs), accept pre-existing conditions with no medical underwriting, and are sold through state or federal exchanges.

### 1.2 Metal Tiers

Every QHP fits into a metal tier based on its actuarial value, which is the average percentage of healthcare costs the plan covers. The consumer pays the rest through deductibles, copays, and coinsurance.

| Metal Tier | Actuarial Value (Plan Pays) |
|---|---|
| Catastrophic | ~55% (available only to under-30 or hardship exempt) |
| Bronze | ~60% |
| Silver | ~70% (eligible for CSR if income qualifies) |
| Gold | ~80% |
| Platinum | ~90% |

**NOTE:** Silver plans are the only tier eligible for Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR). Consumers between 100-250% FPL who select a Silver plan get lower deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums. This is a major selling point.

### 1.3 Financial Assistance

#### Advance Premium Tax Credits (APTC)

APTC reduces the monthly premium based on household income and size. Available to qualifying households based on current FPL thresholds (check current subsidy rules for the applicable plan year). APTC is only available on-exchange.

#### Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR)

CSR lowers deductibles, copays, and out-of-pocket maximums. Only available on Silver plans for households between 100-250% FPL. This does not reduce the premium; it reduces costs when the consumer uses care.

**RULE:** Always run income through the exchange calculator before recommending a plan. If a consumer qualifies for CSR, a Silver plan is almost always the best value even if a Bronze plan has a lower premium.

### 1.4 Exchange Platforms

Each state operates either a state-based exchange (SBE) or uses the Federally Facilitated Marketplace (FFM) via HealthCare.gov. Agents must complete the appropriate certification for each exchange they sell on. FFM certification is not required for state-based exchanges, and vice versa.

**NOTE:** Each state exchange has its own agent certification, training requirements, and enrollment portal. Complete state-specific training before selling in that state.

## 2. Call Opening

The ACA call opening is simpler than MA. There is no TPMO disclaimer, no SOA, and no CMS-mandated verbatim scripts. But you still need to be professional, compliant, and clear.

### 2.1 Identify Yourself

**RULE:** State your full name, that you are a licensed insurance agent, and the agency you represent.

**Say this:**
> "Hi, this is [Agent Name] with [Agency Name]. I am a licensed health insurance agent. Am I speaking with [Client Name]?"

### 2.2 Recording Consent

**RULE:** All calls must be recorded. Get explicit consent before proceeding.

**Say this:**
> "I want to let you know this call may be recorded for quality and compliance purposes. Is that okay with you?"

**DO NOT:** If they decline recording, you cannot proceed with the call.

### 2.3 Confirm Purpose

Unlike MA, there is no formal Scope of Appointment for ACA. However, you should still confirm why they are calling and what they are looking for.

**Say this:**
> "Great. I understand you are looking for health insurance coverage. I am going to ask you some questions to find the best plan for your situation. This does not obligate you to enroll in anything today. Sound good?"

## 3. Eligibility and Enrollment Periods

### 3.1 Who Is Eligible

ACA marketplace coverage is available to U.S. citizens and lawfully present individuals who are not currently incarcerated and who do not have access to affordable employer-sponsored coverage that provides minimum value. There is no medical underwriting; all applicants are accepted regardless of health status.

### 3.2 Enrollment Periods

#### Open Enrollment Period (OEP)

| Plan Year | OEP Dates | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PY 2026 | Nov 1, 2025 - Jan 15, 2026 | Some SBEs extended to Jan 31. Enroll by Dec 15 for Jan 1 effective date. |
| PY 2027 | Nov 1, 2026 - Dec 15, 2026 | Shorter OEP begins. No more Jan 15 deadline. All plans effective Jan 1. |

**NOTE:** For PY 2027, the OEP shortens significantly. Plan your outreach calendar accordingly.

#### Special Enrollment Period (SEP)

Outside of OEP, consumers can only enroll if they have a Qualifying Life Event (QLE). They generally have 60 days from the event to enroll.

| Qualifying Life Event | Details |
|---|---|
| Loss of coverage | Job loss, COBRA expiration, aging off parent's plan at 26, lost Medicaid/CHIP |
| Marriage | At least one spouse must have had prior coverage |
| Birth / Adoption | Adding a new dependent; may allow retroactive coverage |
| Permanent move | Must have had coverage for at least 1 of 60 days before move, or moved from abroad |
| Divorce / Legal separation | Only if it causes loss of coverage |
| Death on plan | If it makes the survivor ineligible for current plan |
| Income change | If newly eligible for APTC or CSR |
| Lost Medicaid/CHIP | Extended SEP windows may apply in some states |

**RULE:** SEP enrollments are heavily audited. Consumers may need to upload documentation to resolve SEP Verification Issues (SVI). If the SVI is not resolved within 30 days of plan selection, coverage can be terminated.

**DO NOT:** Never coach a consumer to misrepresent a qualifying life event. Never fabricate or backdate a QLE. This is fraud.

### 3.3 Effective Dates

| Enrollment Timing | Effective Date |
|---|---|
| OEP: enrolled by Dec 15 | January 1 |
| OEP: enrolled Dec 16 - Jan 15 | February 1 |
| SEP: enrolled before 15th of month | First of the following month |
| SEP: enrolled after 15th of month | First of month after next |
| Birth / Adoption | Date of event (may be retroactive) |

**NOTE:** Coverage is not active until the binder payment is made. The first premium is typically due within 30 days of enrollment. Always remind the consumer to make their first payment.

## 4. Needs Assessment

This is the core of the ACA call. You are matching a plan to their life, not selling a product. Ask the right questions before you ever quote a plan.

### 4.1 Household and Income

**Say this:**
> "To find the right plan and see what financial help you qualify for, I need to ask about your household. How many people are in your tax household? And what is your estimated annual household income for this year?"

**RULE:** Income must be the consumer's best estimate of their current-year income, not last year's tax return. Agents must reinforce that reporting the most accurate estimate is in the consumer's best interest. Underreporting income to maximize APTC can result in a tax liability when they file.

**DO NOT:** Never instruct a consumer to lower their income estimate to get a bigger subsidy. This is a compliance violation and can trigger an IRS repayment.

### 4.2 Coverage Needs

**Say this:**
> "Tell me about your healthcare situation. Do you have any doctors or specialists you need to keep seeing? What prescriptions do you take regularly? Are you expecting any procedures, surgeries, or hospitalizations this year? How often do you typically go to the doctor?"

### 4.3 Budget

**Say this:**
> "What range feels comfortable for a monthly premium? And how much could you handle out of pocket if you needed care, like an ER visit or hospital stay?"

**NOTE:** This question determines whether to lean Bronze (lower premium, higher out-of-pocket) or Silver/Gold (higher premium, lower out-of-pocket). If they qualify for CSR, Silver is almost always the answer regardless of their preference.

### 4.4 Current Coverage Status

**Say this:**
> "What kind of coverage do you have right now, if any? And how did you lose it, or when does it end?"

This determines their enrollment path: OEP, SEP with a QLE, or whether they are currently uninsured and need to wait for the next OEP.

## 5. Plan Presentation and Comparison

Once you know their household, income, health needs, and budget, present plans that fit. Do not dump every option on them. Narrow it to two or three plans and explain why.

### 5.1 What to Cover for Each Plan

For every plan you present, walk through these elements:

| Element | What to Explain |
|---|---|
| Monthly premium | After APTC is applied. Always show the net cost, not full price. |
| Deductible | How much they pay before the plan kicks in. Note whether it applies to all services or just some. |
| Copays / Coinsurance | What they pay at the doctor, specialist, ER, and pharmacy. |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | The most they would pay in a year. After this, the plan covers 100%. |
| Provider network | Is their doctor in-network? Is there a referral requirement? HMO vs PPO vs EPO. |
| Drug formulary | Are their prescriptions covered? What tier? What is the copay? |
| Metal tier | Explain what it means in plain language. |
| CSR (if Silver) | Explain how their deductible and out-of-pocket max are reduced because of their income. |

### 5.2 Provider and Drug Lookup

**RULE:** Always check whether the consumer's doctors and prescriptions are covered by the plan before recommending it. Use the carrier's provider directory and formulary tool. Do not guess.

**DO NOT:** Never tell a consumer their doctor is in-network unless you have verified it. Network directories change. If you cannot verify, say so and tell them how to check.

### 5.3 Comparing Plans

**Say this:**
> "Based on what you told me, here are the two plans that make the most sense. [Plan A] has a lower monthly premium at $[amount] but a higher deductible of $[amount]. [Plan B] costs $[amount] more per month but your deductible is only $[amount] and your copays are lower. Given how often you see the doctor, [Plan B] would likely save you more overall. Which feels like the better fit?"

**NOTE:** Frame the comparison around their specific situation, not abstract features. If they rarely see a doctor, say that. If they have regular prescriptions, show how each plan handles them.

## 6. Consent and Enrollment

### 6.1 Consumer Consent

**RULE:** 45 CFR 155.220(j) requires agents to obtain consumer consent before assisting with marketplace coverage. This includes before searching for an existing application. Consent from a lead generator does not count. The consumer must consent directly.

**Say this:**
> "Before I can access the marketplace system on your behalf, I need your permission to assist you with your application and enrollment. Do I have your consent to help you with your marketplace coverage today?"

**DO NOT:** A consumer checking a box on a lead form is not valid consent under CMS rules. You must obtain consent directly from the consumer.

### 6.2 Identity and Application Information

**RULE:** All eligibility application information must be reviewed and confirmed as accurate by the consumer or their authorized representative. This includes income, household size, SSN, and immigration status.

**Say this:**
> "I am going to walk through your application now. I need to make sure all the information is accurate, so please correct me if anything does not sound right. Let me confirm your full legal name, date of birth, Social Security number, and mailing address."

#### Income Confirmation

**Say this:**
> "You told me your estimated annual household income is $[amount]. This is the number the marketplace will use to calculate your tax credits. It is important that this is your best estimate of what you will actually earn this year. If it turns out to be significantly different, it could affect your taxes. Does $[amount] still sound accurate?"

### 6.3 Plan Selection Confirmation

**RULE:** Before submitting, confirm the consumer understands the plan they selected.

**Say this:**
> "Before I submit your enrollment, let me confirm: you are enrolling in [Carrier Name] [Plan Name], which is a [metal tier] [HMO/PPO/EPO] plan. Your monthly premium after tax credits is $[amount]. Your deductible is $[amount] and your out-of-pocket maximum is $[amount]. Your coverage will begin on [effective date]. Does everything sound correct? Do you want to proceed?"

### 6.4 What You Cannot Do

| Prohibited Action | Why |
|---|---|
| Create a HealthCare.gov or SBE account for the consumer | The consumer must create their own account or you must have their direct consent and presence |
| Use your own email on a consumer's application | Violates CMS rules. Only the consumer's email or their authorized representative's email. |
| Use dummy or placeholder addresses | Violates CMS rules. All information must be real and verified. |
| Retain access to a consumer's account or email | You may assist, but you cannot maintain ongoing access without consent. |
| Make changes to a policy without consumer direction | All updates must be at the consumer's request. |
| Share marketplace login credentials | Your CMS portal credentials are yours only. Never share them. |
| Use automation or scripts on marketplace systems | Requires written CMS permission. |

## 7. Consumer Data and Privacy

ACA agents handle sensitive personal information: SSNs, income, immigration status, health data. CMS takes data protection seriously. Violations can result in immediate termination of your marketplace agreement.

### 7.1 What You Can Do with PII

You may use consumer PII only for: assisting with application, eligibility, plan selection, enrollment, renewal, and disenrollment; assisting with APTC and CSR eligibility; educating consumers on affordability programs; and meeting legal and quality assurance responsibilities.

### 7.2 What You Cannot Do with PII

| Prohibited | Details |
|---|---|
| Use PII for non-marketplace purposes | No marketing, selling to third parties, or using data outside of enrollment assistance |
| Store PII insecurely | Do not keep SSNs, income data, or health information in unencrypted files, notebooks, or unsecured systems |
| Share PII with unauthorized parties | Only share with the consumer, their authorized representative, or as required by law |
| Log into consumer accounts without consent | Direct consumer consent required for each session |

**RULE:** If you suspect any unauthorized access to consumer data or any fraudulent marketplace activity, report it to the Agent/Broker Email Help Desk at CMS immediately.

## 8. After Enrollment

The call does not end when you hit submit. Post-enrollment follow-through determines retention, and retention drives your ACA book.

### 8.1 Confirmation and Next Steps

**Say this:**
> "Your enrollment has been submitted. Here is what happens next. You will receive a confirmation from [exchange/carrier] with your plan details. Your coverage begins on [effective date], but it will not be active until you make your first premium payment. That payment is typically due within 30 days. You will receive your member ID card in the mail within 7 to 14 business days. If you need to see a doctor before it arrives, call [carrier] at [phone number] and they can provide your member ID over the phone."

### 8.2 Binder Payment Reminder

**RULE:** Coverage is NOT active until the first premium payment (binder payment) is made. This is the single most common reason consumers think they are covered and are not.

**Say this:**
> "I want to make sure this is clear: your coverage will not start until you make your first premium payment. You can usually pay online through the carrier's website, by phone, or by mail. I strongly recommend paying as soon as possible so there is no gap in your coverage. Would you like me to walk you through how to make that payment?"

**DO NOT:** Never tell a consumer they are covered the moment enrollment is submitted. They are not covered until the binder payment clears.

### 8.3 Data Matching Issues (DMI)

After enrollment, the marketplace may flag inconsistencies in the consumer's application data, such as income, citizenship, or SSN. These are called Data Matching Issues. If not resolved, the consumer can lose their APTC or have their coverage terminated.

**Say this:**
> "After enrollment, the marketplace may ask you to upload documents to verify some of the information on your application, like your income or citizenship. If you receive a notice about this, it is very important that you respond by the deadline. If you need help uploading documents, call me and I will walk you through it."

### 8.4 SEP Verification Issues (SVI)

If the consumer enrolled through a Special Enrollment Period, the marketplace may require documentation proving the qualifying life event. The consumer has 30 days from plan selection to resolve the SVI. If unresolved, coverage can be terminated retroactively.

**RULE:** Always inform SEP consumers that they may need to upload verification documents. Proactively follow up within a week to make sure this is handled.

### 8.5 Follow-Up Schedule

| Timing | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 1-3 | Confirm the consumer received their enrollment confirmation and knows how to make the binder payment |
| Day 7-10 | Check for any DMI or SVI notices; confirm binder payment was made |
| Day 14-21 | Confirm ID card received; verify consumer can access their online portal |
| Month 2-3 | Check-in: any issues with providers, prescriptions, or billing? |
| 90 days before renewal | Proactive outreach to review plan fit for next year, income changes, life changes |

**Close the call with:**
> "I am going to check in with you in about a week to make sure everything is set up and you have made your first payment. What is the best number and time to reach you? And if you have any questions before then, you can reach me at [your number]. Thank you for choosing [Agency Name]."

## 9. Consumer Experience

ACA consumers are often stressed, confused, or frustrated. Many have been uninsured, lost coverage unexpectedly, or are overwhelmed by options. How you handle the call matters as much as the plan you recommend.

### 9.1 Lead with Empathy, Not Products

Do not jump into plan options. Start by understanding their situation. Many ACA consumers have never had an agent help them before. Take the time to listen.

**DO NOT:** Never open with pricing or a specific plan. Always assess their needs first.

### 9.2 Simplify the Complex

Most consumers do not understand deductibles, coinsurance, metal tiers, or APTC. Use plain language. When you explain a concept, check that they understood it before moving on.

**Example:**
> "A deductible is the amount you pay out of your own pocket before the insurance starts covering costs. So if your deductible is $2,000, you would pay the first $2,000 of medical bills yourself. After that, the plan starts sharing the cost with you. Does that make sense?"

### 9.3 Be Honest About Trade-Offs

Every plan has trade-offs. Bronze is cheap monthly but expensive when you use it. Gold is expensive monthly but cheaper when you use it. Do not pretend there is a perfect plan. Help them choose the trade-off that fits their life.

### 9.4 Never Pressure

If they are not ready to enroll, do not push. Offer to schedule a follow-up. Send them a summary of what you discussed. Give them time to think. Pressured enrollments lead to cancellations and complaints.

**Say this:**
> "I want you to feel confident about this decision. If you need time to think it over, I can send you a summary and we can schedule a follow-up call. There is no pressure."

### 9.5 Own the Relationship

The ACA market runs on retention. Your first enrollment is the beginning, not the end. Clients who hear from you regularly stay. Clients who do not hear from you switch agents or drop coverage. Build the habit of proactive follow-up from day one.

## 10. Common Compliance Mistakes

These are the violations that get agents suspended or terminated from marketplace agreements. Know them and avoid them.

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Enrolling a consumer without their knowledge or consent | Immediate termination of marketplace agreement; potential fraud investigation |
| Using your own email or dummy address on a consumer's application | CMS compliance violation; termination |
| Coaching a consumer to underreport income for a bigger subsidy | IRS repayment liability for the consumer; compliance violation for the agent |
| Fabricating or coaching a QLE for SEP enrollment | Fraud; termination; potential legal action |
| Making application changes without consumer direction | CMS compliance violation |
| Failing to obtain direct consumer consent | Lead-gen consent does not count; enrollment may be voided |
| Storing consumer PII in unsecured locations | Data breach liability; CMS termination |
| Telling a consumer they are covered before binder payment | Consumer shows up to a provider with no active coverage |
| Not completing state-specific exchange training | Not authorized to sell in that state; enrollments may be voided |
| Sharing CMS portal or exchange login credentials | Immediate termination |

## Quick Reference: ACA Call Flow

| Step | What You Do |
|---|---|
| 1. Open | Identify yourself, get recording consent, confirm purpose |
| 2. Eligibility Check | Confirm enrollment period (OEP or SEP with valid QLE), residency, no affordable employer coverage |
| 3. Household & Income | Collect household size, estimated annual income, filing status |
| 4. Needs Assessment | Doctors, prescriptions, anticipated care needs, budget |
| 5. Coverage Status | Current coverage, how/when it ends, QLE details if SEP |
| 6. Plan Presentation | Present 2-3 best-fit plans; cover premium, deductible, copays, OOP max, network, formulary |
| 7. Provider & Drug Check | Verify doctors are in-network and prescriptions are on formulary |
| 8. Comparison & Selection | Compare plans side by side based on their specific situation; let them choose |
| 9. Consent | Get direct consumer consent to assist with marketplace enrollment |
| 10. Application | Walk through application data; confirm all information is accurate with the consumer |
| 11. Plan Confirmation | Read back plan name, carrier, metal tier, premium after APTC, deductible, OOP max, effective date |
| 12. Submit | Submit enrollment; remind about binder payment |
| 13. Close | Provide confirmation details, carrier contact info, payment instructions, follow-up schedule |
